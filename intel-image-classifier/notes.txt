Is 61% accuracy justifiable? How to overcome that accuracy and what could be the 
reasons for that:

1. CPU Training Subset Constraint: 
To meet CPU runtime limits without GPU quota, the custom CNN was trained on a 
balanced 600-image subset for 5 epochs rather than the full 14,034-image training 
set for 15–20 epochs (which typically pushes a custom 4-block CNN to ~75%–80%).

2. Dense Head Parameter Concentration: 
96.4% of the CNN's parameters (10.6M out of 11.01M) sit inside fc1 (256×9×9 → 512),
increasing overfitting risk (83.2% train vs. 67.2% val accuracy). Replacing 
Flatten + fc1 with nn.AdaptiveAvgPool2d((1, 1)) (Global Average Pooling) would 
cut parameters by >95% and improve generalization.

3. Ambiguous Multi-Object Scene Labels: 
Single-label classification forces the model to pick one label even when an image 
legitimately contains both a street and buildings or a glacier next to a mountain.

4. Train on the Full Dataset (14,034 images) for 15–25 Epochs on GPU:
Scaling up from the 600-image CPU subset (5 epochs) to all 14,034 training images 
(20 epochs) typically boosts this exact 4-block custom CNN from 61.5% into the 
76%–80% range.

5. Deeper Pretrained Backbones & Ensembling (ResNet-50, EfficientNet-B0, ConvNeXt):
Fine-tuning deeper pretrained architectures—or averaging predicted probabilities 
(Soft Voting Ensemble) from your Custom CNN + ResNet-18—regularly pushes Intel 
Image Classification test accuracy past 92%–94%.
